Teaching philosophy

My teaching rests on three pillars.

  • Pillar 1

    Inquiry-driven

    I put curiosity and critical thinking first, and questions and mistakes are welcome. In BIS 285, students worked through published studies to see how data become claims.

  • Pillar 2

    Collaborative learning

    Students learn through discussion, teamwork, and interdisciplinary hands-on projects.

  • Pillar 3

    Mentoring

    Teaching at its most individualized: I match each student’s project to their background and goals, and expand their independence as their skills grow.

    Mentees→

AI in the classroom

I treat modern AI as an always-available tutor. That frees our time together in person for what most needs a teacher: formulating problems, reasoning about trade-offs, evaluating evidence, and communicating results. I also teach students to use AI responsibly: to check its output for accuracy and bias, to cite their sources, and to know the limits of these tools.

  1. Forthcoming

    Deep Learning for Medical Image Analysis

    AI for Engineering Education & Research Initiative, University of Washington

    Course designer & instructor Free & open access

    The course moves in three steps: from the fundamentals of medical imaging data, through modern deep-learning architectures, to current research topics. Each module pairs a concise lecture with a hands-on coding notebook on public benchmark datasets.

    1. Step 1

      Fundamentals

      Imaging modalities, data formats, and MRI and CT preprocessing.

    2. Step 2

      Architectures

      Convolutional and transformer architectures for classification and segmentation.

    3. Step 3

      Research topics

      Transfer learning for low-resource settings, uncertainty quantification, and explainable AI for clinical decision support.
